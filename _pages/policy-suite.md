## Problem
Most organizations adopting AI have either no policy at all or a
one-page acceptable-use statement that never mentions prompt
injection, sub-processor training on customer data, or agentic
tools. A coherent suite — acceptable use, vendor risk, and incident
response — is what real governance looks like.

## Approach
- Three policies (Acceptable Use, Vendor Risk Assessment,
  Incident Response) written on a shared eight-section template
  for consistency.
- Every control tied to a recognized framework — OWASP LLM Top 10,
  NIST AI RMF Governance function, and the EU AI Act transparency
  obligations (Article 50).
- Seven AI-specific incident categories were defined that traditional
  IR playbooks don't cover — hallucination, prompt injection,
  model degradation, and more.
- A one-page executive briefing framing the business case.

## Reflection
Writing three policies on one template taught me how much
consistency signals professionalism — predictable structure
makes a suite feel engineered rather than assembled. In v2, I want
to add sectoral overlays (DORA for financial services, HIPAA for
healthcare) and a RACI matrix mapping each policy's roles to a
named governance committee.
